Brent Oil Change Point Analysis: Navigating the Tides of Geopolitics

“In the world of energy, every headline can move markets. But which events truly change the game?”

As a data scientist at Birhan Energies, I was tasked with a challenge: uncover how major geopolitical and OPEC events shape the story of Brent oil prices. This project is more than a technical exercise—it's a journey through data, uncertainty, and the real-world impact of global events on energy markets.

Business Need & Context

The oil market is notoriously volatile. Investors, policymakers, and energy companies all crave clarity: Which events matter most? How do they affect prices? Can we spot the turning points as they happen? Our mission at Birhan Energies is to deliver actionable intelligence—insights that help clients manage risk, seize opportunity, and plan for the future.

  • Investors want to anticipate price swings and maximize returns.
  • Policymakers need to understand market shocks to ensure economic stability and energy security.
  • Energy companies rely on accurate forecasts to plan operations and secure supply chains.

Defining the Data Science Workflow

  • Clarified the business objective: linking major events to structural changes in Brent oil prices.
  • Outlined a workflow: data collection, cleaning, EDA, modeling, evaluation, and communication.

Building the Event Dataset

Initial EDA & Assumptions

  • Documented assumptions: event impact is subjective, correlation ≠ causation, and market reactions may lag events.
Key Insight: Visual inspection revealed that many price jumps and drops align with major events, but not all events have the same impact.

Task 2: Change Point Modeling & Insight Generation

Why Bayesian Change Point Analysis?

Traditional time series models often miss the structural breaks caused by real-world shocks. Bayesian change point detection allows us to statistically identify when the behavior of the price series changes—and to quantify our uncertainty about those moments.

Modeling Approach

  • Transformed prices to log returns for stationarity.
  • Implemented a Bayesian model in PyMC3 to detect a single change point in the mean of log returns.
  • Ran MCMC sampling to estimate the most probable change point and its uncertainty.
  • Compared detected change points to the event list, quantifying the price impact before and after.
Example Finding: In November 2014, the model detected a significant change point coinciding with OPEC's refusal to cut production—leading to a 23% price drop and reduced volatility.

Statistical Rigor

  • Checked model convergence (R-hat ≈ 1.0) and credible intervals.
  • Tested for statistical significance in mean/volatility changes.
  • Associated change points with events within a 6-month window, but noted that not all events cause detectable breaks.
Key Insight: Not every headline moves the market, but some events—especially OPEC decisions and major conflicts—leave a clear statistical fingerprint.

Task 3: Building the Interactive Dashboard

From Analysis to Action

To make these insights accessible, I built a dashboard application using Flask (backend) and React (frontend):

  • Interactive charts show price trends, change points, and event impacts.
  • APIs serve data and model results in real time.
  • Users can filter events, explore statistics, and see how specific events align with price changes.
Key Features: Timeline visualization, change point detection, event impact analysis, and statistical summaries—all in a user-friendly interface.

Conclusions & Recommendations

  • OPEC decisions and major geopolitical events are the most likely to cause structural breaks in oil prices.
  • Bayesian change point analysis provides a principled way to detect and quantify these shifts.
  • Interactive dashboards empower stakeholders to explore, validate, and act on data-driven insights.

For policymakers: Use change point analysis to inform risk management and energy security strategies.
